## B1. Publisher & Standards-Body Authorship Policies

- **ACM Policy on Authorship** — ACM (2023).
  - **Source:** https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/new-acm-policy-on-authorship
  - **Status:** Active policy (recheck after 2026-12); Unverified (no widely-known repo).
  - **Document type:** ACM Publications Policy.
  - **Mechanism:** Defines criteria for authorship in ACM-published works and specifies how generative-AI tools may be used and must be disclosed.
  - **Integration:** Sets the reference for any AI-disclosure copy in venues that follow ACM conventions; states that AI tools cannot be listed as authors and that human authors retain responsibility for AI-assisted content.

- **Nature Portfolio AI Editorial Policy** — Springer Nature (2023).
  - **Source:** https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai
  - **Status:** Active policy (recheck after 2026-12); Unverified (no widely-known repo).
  - **Document type:** Springer Nature editorial policies.
  - **Mechanism:** Editorial policy stating Nature journals' position on LLM use, authorship eligibility, and required disclosure in submitted manuscripts.
  - **Integration:** Canonical "no LLM as author" stance from a top-tier publisher; methods-section disclosure of LLM use is required. Frequently cited as the publisher-side anchor in disclosure debates.

- **COPE Position on Authorship and AI Tools** — COPE (2023).
  - **Source:** https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools
  - **Status:** Active position statement (recheck after 2026-12); Unverified (no widely-known repo).
  - **Document type:** Committee on Publication Ethics position statement.
  - **Mechanism:** Cross-publisher consensus position on the role of AI tools in authorship and the disclosure expectations downstream publishers should adopt.
  - **Integration:** Multi-publisher reference cited by individual journals adopting AI-disclosure language; used when a project needs a non-publisher-specific consensus anchor.
