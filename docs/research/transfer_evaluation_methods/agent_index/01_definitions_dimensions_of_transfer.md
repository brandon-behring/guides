# Definitions + dimensions of transfer (near/far, surface/structural, low-road/high-road)

Sub-area `A1` of the Research Plan: Transfer evaluation methods for interview-prep guides dossier. 9 entries.

## A1.1 Situated Learning and Education

- **Situated Learning and Education** — Anderson, Reder & Simon (1996); *Educational Researcher, 25(4), 5-11*. [pitfall]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X025004005
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Reviews and contests four situated-learning claims (including knowledge does not transfer between tasks), citing evidence that abstract training does transfer and that strong situativity claims are overstated.
  - **Result:** Cognitivist counterpoint anchoring the situated-vs-cognitive transfer debate from the cognitive side.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0010

## A1.3 Rethinking Transfer: A Simple Proposal with Multiple Implications

- **Rethinking Transfer: A Simple Proposal with Multiple Implications** — Bransford & Schwartz (1999); *Review of Research in Education, 24, 61-100*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0091732X024001061
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Argues the dominant direct-application view of transfer is too narrow and proposes a preparation-for-future-learning (PFL) lens that credits learners for being prepared to learn in a new setting.
  - **Result:** Reconceptualizes transfer and seeds the PFL assessment paradigm bridging A1 and A4.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0009

## A1.4 How People Learn: Brain, Mind, Experience, and School (Expanded Edition)

- **How People Learn: Brain, Mind, Experience, and School (Expanded Edition)** — Bransford, Brown & Cocking (eds.) (2000); *National Academies Press (NRC)*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.17226/9853
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Synthesizes cognitive-science research on learning and transfer, arguing that transfer depends on degree of mastery, understanding versus memorization, time-on-task, and the representation of the original learning context.
  - **Result:** Foundational NRC synthesis that frames the modern transfer agenda and is the canonical entry point for the dossier.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0005

## A1.5 Schema Induction and Analogical Transfer

- **Schema Induction and Analogical Transfer** — Gick & Holyoak (1983); *Cognitive Psychology, 15(1), 1-38*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285%2883%2990002-6
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Experiments show analogical transfer is cued by surface-semantic features but facilitated by inducing a general schema from multiple analogs, separating surface from structural similarity.
  - **Result:** Landmark empirical basis for the surface/structural distinction and schema-mediated far transfer.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0008

## A1.6 On Claims That Answer the Wrong Questions

- **On Claims That Answer the Wrong Questions** — Greeno (1997); *Educational Researcher, 26(1), 5-17*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X026001005
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Replies to Anderson et al.
  - **Result:** by arguing that what transfers is patterns of participation across situations, reframing transfer from a situative rather than purely cognitive standpoint.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0011

## A1.7 Toward a Model of Transfer as Sense-Making

- **Toward a Model of Transfer as Sense-Making** — Nokes-Malach & Mestre (2013); *Educational Psychologist, 48(3), 184-207*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.807556
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Proposes a process model in which transfer is a cycle of constructing a context representation, generating a solution, and evaluating whether it makes sense, integrating cognitive and situative accounts.
  - **Result:** Modern integrative framework reconciling the cognitive/situative split and updating the dimensions discussion.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0012

## A1.8 Rocky Roads to Transfer: Rethinking Mechanisms of a Neglected Phenomenon

- **Rocky Roads to Transfer: Rethinking Mechanisms of a Neglected Phenomenon** — Salomon & Perkins (1989); *Educational Psychologist, 24(2), 113-142*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep2402_1
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Distinguishes low-road transfer (automatic triggering of well-practiced behavior in similar contexts) from high-road transfer (deliberate, mindful abstraction), with forward- and backward-reaching variants.
  - **Result:** Canonical source for the low-road/high-road dimension named in the sub-area scope.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0007

## A1.9 The Influence of Improvement in One Mental Function upon the Efficiency of Other Functions (I)

- **The Influence of Improvement in One Mental Function upon the Efficiency of Other Functions (I)** — Thorndike & Woodworth (1901); *Psychological Review, 8(3), 247-261*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1037/h0074898
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Improvement in one mental function transfers to another only insofar as the two functions share identical elements, undercutting the doctrine of formal discipline and general mental faculties.
  - **Result:** Origin point of transfer research and the identical-elements account that every later taxonomy responds to.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0006
