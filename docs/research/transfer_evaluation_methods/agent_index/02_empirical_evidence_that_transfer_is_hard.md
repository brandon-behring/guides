## A2.1 The Import of Knowledge Export: Connecting Findings and Theories of Transfer of Learning

- **The Import of Knowledge Export: Connecting Findings and Theories of Transfer of Learning** — Day & Goldstone (2012); *Educational Psychologist, 47(3)*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2012.696438
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Reviews why transfer appears so rare in the lab yet pervasive in everyday expertise, arguing that methodological framing and overly literal similarity criteria drive much of the documented pessimism.
  - **Result:** The leading reframing/critique of the transfer-is-rare canon, balancing the pessimism sources.
  - **Status:** Unverified

## A2.3 Analogical Problem Solving

- **Analogical Problem Solving** — Gick & Holyoak (1980); *Cognitive Psychology, 12(3)*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285%2880%2990013-4
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Across five experiments using Duncker radiation problem, subjects who first read a structurally analogous military story rarely produced the convergence solution spontaneously, but did so far more often once given an explicit hint to use the story.
  - **Result:** Foundational demonstration that spontaneous analogical transfer fails without a retrieval cue, anchoring the transfer-is-hard canon.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0019

## A2.4 Cognitive Training: A Field in Search of a Phenomenon

- **Cognitive Training: A Field in Search of a Phenomenon** — Gobet & Sala (2023); *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 18(1)*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221091830
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Reviewing multiple domains (working memory, music, chess, exergames), the authors argue the overall far-transfer effect is null with little true between-domain variability, questioning whether the phenomenon exists at all.
  - **Result:** Synthesizes the entire cognitive-training program into a strong statement of the pessimistic position.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0023

## A2.5 Cognition in Practice: Mind, Mathematics and Culture in Everyday Life

- **Cognition in Practice: Mind, Mathematics and Culture in Everyday Life** — Lave (1988); *Cambridge University Press*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511609268
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Adults who solve arithmetic problems fluently while grocery shopping or dieting fail equivalent school-format problems, showing that competence is bound to setting rather than transferring as decontextualized skill.
  - **Result:** Situated-cognition critique arguing transfer failures reflect that cognition is constituted by its activity context.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0020

## A2.6 Working Memory Training Does Not Improve Performance on Measures of Intelligence or Other Measures of Far Transfer: Evidence From a Meta-Analytic Review

- **Working Memory Training Does Not Improve Performance on Measures of Intelligence or Other Measures of Far Transfer: Evidence From a Meta-Analytic Review** — Melby-Lervag, Redick & Hulme (2016); *Perspectives on Psychological Science, 11(4)*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635612
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Meta-analysis of working-memory training studies found reliable near-transfer gains but no convincing evidence of far transfer to nonverbal ability, reading, or arithmetic when compared to treated controls.
  - **Result:** Canonical meta-analytic verdict that working-memory training does not produce far transfer.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0021

## A2.7 Video Game Training Does Not Enhance Cognitive Ability: A Comprehensive Meta-Analytic Investigation

- **Video Game Training Does Not Enhance Cognitive Ability: A Comprehensive Meta-Analytic Investigation** — Sala, Tatlidil & Gobet (2018); *Psychological Bulletin, 144(2)*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000139
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** A comprehensive meta-analysis found the far-transfer effect of video-game training on cognitive ability to be essentially null once methodological quality and active controls were accounted for.
  - **Result:** Extends the far-transfer-fails verdict from working memory to the popular game-training literature.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0022
