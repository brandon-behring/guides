## A5.1 The Effects of Statistical Training on Thinking about Everyday Problems

- **The Effects of Statistical Training on Thinking about Everyday Problems** — Fong, Krantz & Nisbett (1986); *Cognitive Psychology, 18(3)*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285%2886%2990001-0
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Brief training in the law of large numbers increased both the frequency and quality of statistical reasoning on everyday problems in domains where the rule was never explicitly taught, showing domain-independent transfer of an abstract rule.
  - **Result:** Landmark evidence that statistical-method reasoning CAN transfer far when taught as an abstract pragmatic rule.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0025

## A5.2 From One Language to the Next: Applications of Analogical Transfer for Programming Education

- **From One Language to the Next: Applications of Analogical Transfer for Programming Education** — Kao, Matlen & Weintrop (2022); *ACM Transactions on Computing Education, 22(4)*. [methodology]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3487051
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Maps cognitive-psychology theories of analogical transfer onto programming-language learning, proposing how comparison and alignment techniques could support teaching multiple languages.
  - **Result:** Bridges the analogical-transfer literature directly into CS-education practice for learning across languages.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0029

## A5.3 The Transfer Effect of Computational Thinking (CT)-STEM: A Systematic Literature Review and Meta-Analysis

- **The Transfer Effect of Computational Thinking (CT)-STEM: A Systematic Literature Review and Meta-Analysis** — Li & Oon (2024); *International Journal of STEM Education, 11*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-024-00498-z
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Across 37 studies and 7,832 students the authors report a moderate overall transfer effect, with moderate effects for both near and far transfer of computational thinking, moderated by educational level and instructional factors.
  - **Result:** Recent quantitative synthesis showing computational-thinking instruction does produce measurable transfer in STEM, a counterpoint to the pessimism literature.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0030

## A5.4 It's Like Python But: Towards Supporting Transfer of Programming Language Knowledge

- **It's Like Python But: Towards Supporting Transfer of Programming Language Knowledge** — Shrestha, Barik & Parnin (2018); *IEEE VL/HCC 2018*. [domain_application]
  - **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09008
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** A Transfer Tutor that explained R syntax and semantics in terms of programmers existing Python knowledge was used to study how prior-language knowledge scaffolds — and sometimes misleads — learning of a new language.
  - **Result:** Concrete tool-based study of near transfer of programming-language knowledge across Python and R.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0027

## A5.5 Here We Go Again: Why Is It Difficult for Developers to Learn Another Programming Language?

- **Here We Go Again: Why Is It Difficult for Developers to Learn Another Programming Language?** — Shrestha et al. (2020); *ICSE 2020*. [pitfall]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3377811.3380352
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Analysis of Stack Overflow questions across 18 languages plus interviews found hundreds of cases of negative transfer, where faulty assumptions carried over from a known language interfered with learning a new one.
  - **Result:** Distinguished-paper evidence of negative/interference transfer between programming languages — the core pitfall for multi-language learners.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0028

## A5.6 Empirical Studies of Programming Knowledge

- **Empirical Studies of Programming Knowledge** — Soloway & Ehrlich (1984); *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering, SE-10(5)*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.1984.5010283
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Expert programmers rely on reusable programming plans and rules of programming discourse; violating these conventions degraded expert comprehension toward novice levels, evidencing plan-based transferable knowledge.
  - **Result:** Foundational account of programming plans as the unit of transferable programming expertise.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_transfer_evaluation_methods_0026
