## A2.3 High-Impact Educational Practices: What They Are, Who Has Access to Them, and Why They Matter

- **High-Impact Educational Practices: What They Are, Who Has Access to Them, and Why They Matter** — Kuh (2008); *AAC&U (Association of American Colleges and Universities)*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://assess.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2019-02/kuh_2008.pdf
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Kuh identifies 10 high-impact educational practices including capstone courses/projects; recommends every undergraduate participate in at least two high-impact activities across their program.
  - **Result:** Foundational AAC&U argument that capstones are one of the canonical high-impact practices; the anchor for treating capstone as a credentialing structure rather than an optional culminating course.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_capstone_portfolio_assessment_0001

## A2.7 A systematic literature review of capstone courses in software engineering

- **A systematic literature review of capstone courses in software engineering** — Tenhunen et al. (2023); *Information and Software Technology, 159, 107191*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2023.107191
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Systematic review of 127 SE capstone-course articles (2007-2022) synthesizes a taxonomy across duration, team size, client/project sources, project implementation, and student assessment; finds the modal course is one semester with 4-5 person teams building proof-of-concept software for an external client.
  - **Result:** See Mechanism.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_capstone_portfolio_assessment_0021
