## A5.1 How to Create and Use Rubrics for Formative Assessment and Grading

- **How to Create and Use Rubrics for Formative Assessment and Grading** — Brookhart (2013); *ASCD*. [methodology]
  - **Source:** https://archive.org/details/howtocreateuseru0000broo
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Identifies two essential rubric components — criteria that target learning (not tasks) and clear descriptions of performance across a quality continuum — and argues analytic rubrics are better suited to formative use because students can see which dimensions need work.
  - **Result:** See Mechanism.
  - **Status:** Unverified

## A5.2 A Coefficient of Agreement for Nominal Scales

- **A Coefficient of Agreement for Nominal Scales** — Cohen (1960); *Educational and Psychological Measurement, 20(1), 37-46*. [methodology]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/001316446002000104
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Introduces kappa as a chance-corrected agreement coefficient for nominal scales, critiquing raw percent agreement for failing to account for the probability that raters coincide by guessing.
  - **Result:** See Mechanism.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_capstone_portfolio_assessment_0013

## A5.3 Issues in measuring reliability: Correlation versus percentage of agreement

- **Issues in measuring reliability: Correlation versus percentage of agreement** — Hayes & Hatch (1999); *Written Communication, 16(3), 354-367*. [pitfall]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/0741088399016003001
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Demonstrates that correlation-based and percent-agreement reliability indices can diverge sharply, and that many portfolio-assessment studies report reliability statistics inappropriate to the scoring design they used.
  - **Result:** See Mechanism.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_capstone_portfolio_assessment_0017

## A5.4 The use of scoring rubrics: Reliability, validity and educational consequences

- **The use of scoring rubrics: Reliability, validity and educational consequences** — Jonsson & Svingby (2007); *Educational Research Review, 2(2), 130-144*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2007.05.002
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Review of 75 studies finds rubric scoring of performance assessments is most reliable when rubrics are analytic, topic-specific, and complemented with exemplars and/or rater training; rubrics do not automatically yield validity but can support it under a richer validity framework.
  - **Result:** See Mechanism.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_capstone_portfolio_assessment_0010

## A5.5 Reliability in Content Analysis: Some Common Misconceptions and Recommendations

  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2004.tb00738.x
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Clarifies misconceptions about reliability coefficients in coding tasks and recommends alpha as a generalized chance-corrected agreement statistic that handles any number of raters, any measurement level (nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio), and missing data.

## A5.6 The use of scoring rubrics for formative assessment purposes revisited: A review

- **The use of scoring rubrics for formative assessment purposes revisited: A review** — Panadero & Jonsson (2013); *Educational Research Review, 9, 129-144*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2013.01.002
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Analyzes 21 studies on formative rubric use and identifies several mediating mechanisms — transparency of expectations, supported self-assessment, feedback scaffolding — through which rubrics improve student performance and self-regulation.

## A5.7 A review of rubric use in higher education

## A5.8 A Comparison of Consensus, Consistency, and Measurement Approaches to Estimating Interrater Reliability

- **A Comparison of Consensus, Consistency, and Measurement Approaches to Estimating Interrater Reliability** — Stemler (2004); *Practical Assessment, Research, and Evaluation, 9(4)*. [methodology]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.7275/96jp-xz07
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Argues interrater reliability is not one concept but three families — consensus (percent agreement, kappa), consistency (correlations), and measurement (G-theory) estimates — each with different assumptions, interpretations, and appropriate uses.
  - **Result:** See Mechanism.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_capstone_portfolio_assessment_0015
