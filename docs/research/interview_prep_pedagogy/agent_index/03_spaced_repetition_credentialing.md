# Spaced Repetition + Testing Effect in Credentialing Contexts

Peer-reviewed evidence on spaced repetition, retrieval practice, and Anki-style flashcards in professional credentialing — foundational learning-science reviews plus an applied medical-school study. Industry credentialing methodology lives in `01_highstakes_testprep.md`; classical problem-solving theory lives in `04_expert_problemsolving.md`.

## C1. Foundational learning-techniques and testing-effect evidence

- **Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques** — Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (PSPI 2013).
  - **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1529100612453266
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Comprehensive review classifying ten widely-used study techniques (practice testing, distributed practice, elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, interleaved practice, summarization, highlighting, keyword mnemonics, imagery, and re-reading) by utility level.
  - **Result:** Identifies practice testing (retrieval practice) and distributed practice as the two high-utility techniques most other techniques pale next to — the foundational synthesis cited by virtually every modern learning-science review.
  - **Status:** (no widely-known repo) Unverified.

- **The Power of Testing Memory** — Roediger & Karpicke (PPS 2006).
  - **Source:** http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PPS.pdf
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Review article summarizing experimental evidence on the testing effect — that retrieval practice strengthens memory more than restudy.
  - **Result:** Canonical review establishing the testing effect as a robust and educationally important phenomenon; cited heavily in Dunlosky et al. (2013).
  - **Status:** (no widely-known repo) Unverified.

## C2. Spaced repetition applied to medical-school credentialing

- **Academic and Wellness Outcomes Associated with Anki Use in Medical School** — Wothe et al. (JMECD 2023).
  - **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/23821205231173289
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Survey-based study correlating Anki usage with academic and wellness outcomes among medical students.
  - **Result:** Provides applied evidence within a high-stakes credentialing context (USMLE-track medical school) on the relationship between Anki adoption and outcomes — the closest peer-reviewed analog to "Anki for technical-interview prep."
  - **Status:** (no widely-known repo) Unverified.

---

**Notes:** Dunlosky et al. (2013) and Roediger & Karpicke (2006) provide the cognitive-psychology foundation; Wothe et al. (2023) is the applied-to-credentialing bridge. Cohort-outcome effect sizes from Wothe et al. should be re-verified against the primary PDF before being asserted in synthesis material.
