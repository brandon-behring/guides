# Expert Problem-Solving and Pattern Recognition

Classical and contemporary works on how experts solve problems and how novices acquire expert-like pattern recognition — Pólya's heuristic, Schoenfeld's metacognition framework, CS-education evidence, and the industry-canon problem-pattern book that downstream tech-interview prep is built on. Empirical interview research lives in `02_interview_research.md`; mock-practice / verbalization methodology lives in `05_mock_practice.md`.

## D1. Classical problem-solving theory

- **How to Solve It** — Pólya (Princeton University Press 1945; 2nd ed. 1957).
  - **Source:** https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691164076/how-to-solve-it
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Book-length exposition of a four-phase problem-solving heuristic (understand the problem, devise a plan, carry out the plan, look back), with worked mathematical examples.
  - **Result:** Foundational four-step heuristic that downstream CS-interview frameworks (clarify → plan → code → verify) descend from; one of the most widely-cited works on the methodology of problem solving.
  - **Status:** (no widely-known repo) Unverified.

- **Mathematical Problem Solving** — Schoenfeld (Academic Press 1985).
  - **Source:** https://books.google.com/books/about/Mathematical_Problem_Solving.html?id=0cbSBQAAQBAJ
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Research monograph extending Pólya with empirical investigation of metacognition, heuristics, beliefs, and control as components of expert problem solving.
  - **Result:** Establishes metacognition and beliefs about problem-solving as load-bearing components of expert performance — Pólya's heuristic alone is insufficient without the metacognitive layer.
  - **Status:** (no widely-known repo) Unverified.

## D2. CS-education evidence and industry-canon prep

- **Studying the Novice Programmer** — Soloway & Spohrer (eds.) (Lawrence Erlbaum 1989).
  - **Source:** https://www.routledge.com/Studying-the-Novice-Programmer/Soloway-Spohrer/p/book/9780805800036
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Edited volume of empirical CS-education studies on how novice programmers acquire and structure programming knowledge — chunking, plan-based reasoning, common bug categories.
  - **Result:** Establishes the empirical basis for chunking and plan-based pattern recognition as the mechanism by which novices grow into expert programmers; cited by downstream "interview pattern" frameworks even when uncited.
  - **Status:** (no widely-known repo) Unverified.

- **Cracking the Coding Interview** — McDowell (CareerCup 2015; 6th ed.).
  - **Source:** https://www.crackingthecodinginterview.com/
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Industry-canon self-published book of curated coding-interview problems organized by topic (arrays, strings, linked lists, trees, graphs, recursion, DP, etc.) with worked solutions.
  - **Result:** Defines the dominant practitioner-facing pattern taxonomy that contemporary tech-interview prep is built around — the data-structure-by-data-structure organization scheme is McDowell's by default.
  - **Status:** (industry resource) Unverified.

---

**Notes:** D1 is academic. D2 mixes academic (Soloway & Spohrer 1989) and industry-canon (McDowell, self-published via CareerCup). McDowell is widely read but not peer-reviewed; treat as a practitioner reference rather than as a research source.
