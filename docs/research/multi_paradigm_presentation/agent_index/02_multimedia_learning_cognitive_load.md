## A2.1 Meta-analysis of the modality effect

- **Meta-analysis of the modality effect** — Ginns (2005); *Learning and Instruction, 15(4), 313-331*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2005.07.001
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Meta-analytic synthesis of modality-effect experiments confirms learners perform better with spoken narration + graphics than printed text + graphics, with moderators around pacing, complexity, and system pacing.
  - **Result:** Definitive quantitative defense (and boundary-mapping) of the modality principle — the empirical anchor when arguing for narrated screencasts over text-heavy slides.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0027

## A2.2 The Expertise Reversal Effect

- **The Expertise Reversal Effect** — Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003); *Educational Psychologist, 38(1), 23-31*. [principle]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Review of empirical work showing instructional techniques that benefit novices (e.g., worked examples, full explanations) can hinder more knowledgeable learners — what helps depends on prior expertise, so designs must fade scaffolds.
  - **Result:** Critical boundary condition on every multimedia design principle — prevents naive application of redundancy/modality/signaling rules across an audience with mixed expertise.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0024

## A2.3 Nine Ways to Reduce Cognitive Load in Multimedia Learning

- **Nine Ways to Reduce Cognitive Load in Multimedia Learning** — Mayer & Moreno (2003); *Educational Psychologist, 38(1), 43-52*. [principle]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_6
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Examines five overload scenarios in multimedia learning and proposes one or two theory-based load-reduction techniques per scenario (off-loading, segmenting, pretraining, weeding, signaling, aligning, eliminating redundancy, synchronizing, individualizing), with empirical results testing each.
  - **Result:** Canonical short list of presentation-design moves tied directly to cognitive-load reduction; the most-cited single article underpinning code-vs-prose layout decisions.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0020

## A2.5 Cognitive Principles of Multimedia Learning: The Role of Modality and Contiguity

- **Cognitive Principles of Multimedia Learning: The Role of Modality and Contiguity** — Moreno & Mayer (1999); *Journal of Educational Psychology, 91(2), 358-368*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.91.2.358
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Two experiments show learners produce better retention and transfer when narration accompanies animation (modality effect) and when corresponding visual and verbal material is presented simultaneously (temporal-contiguity effect).
  - **Result:** Primary empirical source for modality + contiguity principles — directly relevant to deciding when prose should be audio narration vs. on-screen text alongside diagrams/code.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0021

## A2.7 A Meta-analysis of the Segmenting Effect

- **A Meta-analysis of the Segmenting Effect** — Rey et al. (2019); *Educational Psychology Review, 31(2), 389-419*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Meta-analysis of segmenting (chunking + user-paced) multimedia material; reports a positive overall effect on retention and transfer with moderators including learner control, pacing, and complexity.
  - **Result:** Best meta-analytic evidence base for the segmenting/chunking principle that underwrites how to slice long code walkthroughs or worked examples into pause-able units.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0025

## A2.8 A meta-analysis of how signaling affects learning with media

- **A meta-analysis of how signaling affects learning with media** — Schneider, Beege, Nebel & Rey (2018); *Educational Research Review, 23, 1-24*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.11.001
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Meta-analysis of 44 effect sizes from 29 experiments (N=2726) finds signaling cues produce a moderate positive effect on learning outcomes (d=0.38), moderated by study quality, prior-knowledge controls, and outcome reliability.
  - **Result:** Quantitative basis for the signaling principle (cueing/highlighting) — critical for presentation choices like syntax highlighting, callouts, and diagram annotations.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0026
