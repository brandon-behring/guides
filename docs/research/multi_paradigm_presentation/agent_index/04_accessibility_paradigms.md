## A4.1 Inter-letter spacing, inter-word spacing, and font with dyslexia-friendly features: testing text readability in people with and without dyslexia

- **Inter-letter spacing, inter-word spacing, and font with dyslexia-friendly features: testing text readability in people with and without dyslexia** — Galuschka et al. (2020); *Annals of Dyslexia, 70*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11881-020-00194-x
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Tests reading speed and accuracy with increased letter spacing, word spacing, and a dyslexia-targeted font in dyslexic and typical readers; finds no general advantage for any condition, partially contradicting Zorzi et al.
  - **Result:** (2012).
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0017

## A4.2 ColorBrewer.org: An Online Tool for Selecting Colour Schemes for Maps

- **ColorBrewer.org: An Online Tool for Selecting Colour Schemes for Maps** — Harrower & Brewer (2003); *Cartographic Journal, 40(1)*. [accessibility]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1559/152304003100010929
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Describes ColorBrewer, an evidence-based tool for selecting sequential, diverging, and qualitative map color schemes (3-12 classes) tuned for perceptual effectiveness, colorblind safety, photocopy reproduction, and display environment.
  - **Result:** Foundational reference for principled choice of categorical/sequential/diverging palettes; widely reused outside cartography in scientific visualization.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0014

## A4.4 Good fonts for dyslexia

- **Good fonts for dyslexia** — Rello & Baeza-Yates (2013); *ASSETS 2013 (15th ACM SIGACCESS Conf. on Computers and Accessibility)*. [accessibility]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1145/2513383.2513447
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** First eye-tracking study of font type on dyslexic reading (48 participants, 12 fonts); sans-serif, monospaced, and roman styles significantly outperform serif, proportional, and italic; specialized dyslexia fonts like OpenDyslexic are not faster than Arial or Helvetica.
  - **Result:** The empirical reference debunking the claim that specialized dyslexia fonts outperform standard sans-serif typefaces; widely cited ASSETS paper.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0015

## A4.5 MathPlayer: web-based math accessibility

- **MathPlayer: web-based math accessibility** — Soiffer (2005); *ASSETS 2005 (7th International ACM SIGACCESS Conf.)*. [tooling]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1145/1090785.1090831
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Describes MathPlayer, a browser plug-in that renders MathML visually and converts it to speech for screen readers (JAWS, Window-Eyes); supports synchronized highlighting, expression magnification, and Braille math output.
  - **Result:** Foundational ASSETS paper on web-based math accessibility; lineage source for MathCAT/MathJax accessibility infrastructure used today.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0018

## A4.6 Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2

- **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2** — W3C (2023); *W3C Recommendation (5 October 2023)*. [accessibility]
  - **Source:** https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Defines 13 guidelines under POUR principles (Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust) with 86 testable success criteria across A/AA/AAA conformance levels; adds 9 new criteria over 2.1 addressing focus appearance, target size, dragging movements, and cognitive accessibility.
  - **Result:** Current canonical W3C web accessibility standard, now also ISO/IEC 40500:2025; the load-bearing reference for any accessibility-as-constraint discussion.
  - **Status:** Unverified (standards / vendor / docs page)
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0011

## A4.8 Points of view: Color blindness

- **Points of view: Color blindness** — Wong (2011); *Nature Methods, 8(6), 441*. [accessibility]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.1618
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Introduces an eight-color qualitative palette (black, orange, sky blue, bluish green, yellow, blue, vermillion, reddish purple) selected to remain distinguishable under all three major forms of color vision deficiency; explicitly recommended by Nature journals.
  - **Result:** Canonical Nature Methods reference for colorblind-safe categorical palettes; the citation paired with the Okabe-Ito set.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0013

## A4.9 Extra-large letter spacing improves reading in dyslexia

- **Extra-large letter spacing improves reading in dyslexia** — Zorzi et al. (2012); *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 109(28)*. [accessibility]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1205566109
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Demonstrates in 54 Italian and 40 French dyslexic children that simply increasing inter-letter spacing (+18% body size) improves reading speed and accuracy on-the-fly without training, with no parallel benefit for typical readers.
  - **Result:** The PNAS paper providing the strongest empirical case for letter-spacing as a dyslexia-readability intervention; standard reference (with caveats noted in later mixed replications).
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0016
