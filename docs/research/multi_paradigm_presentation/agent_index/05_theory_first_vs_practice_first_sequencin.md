# Theory-first vs practice-first sequencing

Sub-area `A5` of the Research Plan: Multi-paradigm presentation + UDL (Universal Design for Learning) dossier. 8 entries.

## A5.1 A Meta-Analytic Review of the Concrete-Representational-Abstract Math Approach

- **A Meta-Analytic Review of the Concrete-Representational-Abstract Math Approach** — Ebner, MacDonald, Grekov & Aspiranti (2025); *Learning Disability Quarterly (online 2025)*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/09388982241292299
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** 30 single-case-design studies synthesized via Tau-BC meta-analysis show a large overall effect for CRA sequencing in math learning; moderator analysis weakly favors non-integrated (strictly sequential) over integrated CRA variants.
  - **Result:** Recent quantitative anchor for the concrete-to-abstract sequencing tradition — complements PF/PFL with a different (CRA / Bruner-lineage) operationalization of practice-first ordering.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0035

## A5.2 Productive Failure

- **Productive Failure** — Kapur (2008); *Cognition and Instruction, 26(3), 379-424*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07370000802212669
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Classroom study with 7th-grade math: students who first attempted ill-structured complex problems without scaffolds underperformed initially but outperformed direct-instruction peers on well-structured and transfer problems after subsequent instruction.
  - **Result:** Original productive-failure paper — defines the construct that anchors the practice-first sequencing literature.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0030

## A5.3 Designing for Productive Failure

- **Designing for Productive Failure** — Kapur & Bielaczyc (2012); *Journal of the Learning Sciences, 21(1), 45-83*. [principle]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2011.591717
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Articulates a two-phase generation-then-consolidation design (problem-solving before instruction) and reports three studies showing PF students outperformed direct-instruction peers on conceptual understanding and transfer at no cost to procedural fluency.
  - **Result:** Provides the design-principle layer (not just the phenomenon) for sequencing decisions — what makes a generation activity actually productive.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0031

## A5.4 Knowing what you don't know makes failure productive

- **Knowing what you don't know makes failure productive** — Loibl & Rummel (2014); *Learning and Instruction, 34, 74-85*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2014.08.004
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Experiment shows that simply having students attempt problems before instruction is insufficient; benefits emerge only when the subsequent instruction explicitly builds on students typical erroneous solutions, making knowledge gaps salient.
  - **Result:** Important boundary condition on sequencing claims — clarifies that the telling phase must reference learners productive failures for the order to pay off.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0034

## A5.5 Towards a Theory of When and How Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Supports Learning

- **Towards a Theory of When and How Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Supports Learning** — Loibl, Roll & Rummel (2017); *Educational Psychology Review, 29(4), 693-715*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9398-2
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Reviews PS-I research and proposes mechanisms by which initial problem-solving supports later instruction: activating prior knowledge, becoming aware of knowledge gaps, and noticing deep features — explaining when sequencing helps and when it does not.
  - **Result:** Theoretical scaffolding for the PS-I/PFL literature; gives the cognitive mechanisms that explain why and when practice-first sequencing works.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0033

## A5.6 A Time for Telling

- **A Time for Telling** — Schwartz & Bransford (1998); *Cognition and Instruction, 16(4), 475-522*. [theory]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1604_4
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Three studies show that analyzing contrasting cases first generates differentiated knowledge structures that prepare learners to benefit from a subsequent lecture or text — establishing the time-for-telling framework for sequencing.
  - **Result:** Foundational primary source for the practice-first / contrasting-cases sequencing argument; the canonical citation for why explanations land better after learner-generated differentiation.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0028

## A5.7 Inventing to Prepare for Future Learning: The Hidden Efficiency of Encouraging Original Student Production in Statistics Instruction

- **Inventing to Prepare for Future Learning: The Hidden Efficiency of Encouraging Original Student Production in Statistics Instruction** — Schwartz & Martin (2004); *Cognition and Instruction, 22(2), 129-184*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci2202_1
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Four 9th-grade statistics experiments show that invention activities (which appear inefficient on immediate tests) couple with subsequent lectures to produce strong transfer, procedural skill, and ability to evaluate data — operationalizing preparation for future learning.
  - **Result:** Empirical workhorse for the PFL framework; demonstrates why hidden-efficiency assessments matter when comparing theory-first vs. practice-first sequences.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0029

## A5.8 When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Works: Evidence for Productive Failure

- **When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Works: Evidence for Productive Failure** — Sinha & Kapur (2021); *Review of Educational Research, 91(5), 761-798*. [evidence]
  - **Source:** https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Meta-analysis of 53 studies / 166 comparisons (N>12,000) finds problem-solving-before-instruction yields significantly better conceptual understanding and transfer than instruction-first (d=0.36; up to 0.58 at high design fidelity), without compromising procedural knowledge.
  - **Result:** Strongest single piece of quantitative evidence comparing theory-first vs. practice-first sequencing across domains and grade levels — load-bearing citation for the whole sub-area.
  - **Status:** Unverified
  - **Evidence:** ev_multi_paradigm_presentation_0032
