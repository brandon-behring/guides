# Cognitive Load and Multimedia Learning

Synthesis of cognitive-load theory (Sweller and successors) and the cognitive theory of multimedia learning (Mayer). Together these lines govern instructional design for technical material with mixed text and figures.

## B1. Cognitive Load Theory

- **Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning** — Sweller (Cognitive Science 1988).
  - **Source:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Analyzes conventional problem-solving practice in terms of working-memory load and argues that means-ends search interferes with schema acquisition.
  - **Result:** Foundational paper for cognitive-load theory; introduces the distinction between solving problems and learning from them; motivates worked-example instruction.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **Expertise Reversal Effect and Its Implications for Learner-Tailored Instruction** — Kalyuga (Educational Psychology Review 2007; based on Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller 2003).
  - **Source:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-007-9054-3
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Reviews the expertise reversal effect: instructional supports beneficial for novices (worked examples, redundant verbal explanations) lose effectiveness or harm learning at higher expertise levels.
  - **Result:** Documents that instructional design must adapt to learner expertise; provides the conceptual basis for fading scaffolds (worked → partial → solo) as expertise develops.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

## B2. Multimedia Learning (Mayer's CTML)

- **Multimedia Learning (2nd ed.)** — Mayer (Cambridge University Press 2009).
  - **Source:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/multimedia-learning/A0BEF8AB04EC11FCD2C4A89C5DFFB1A2
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Book-length synthesis presenting the cognitive theory of multimedia learning (CTML) and the empirically supported design principles.
  - **Result:** Canonical statement of CTML and the multimedia design principles (coherence, signaling, redundancy, spatial/temporal contiguity, segmenting, pre-training, modality, personalization, voice, image).
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning (2nd ed.)** — Mayer ed. (Cambridge University Press 2014).
  - **Source:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/09E09224829AB8D3D327EF8A0E9B5288
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Edited handbook collecting contributed chapters on multimedia-learning theory, individual principles, boundary conditions, and applied studies across domains.
  - **Result:** Comprehensive reference for the multimedia-learning field; expands and updates the 2009 monograph with domain applications and emerging boundary conditions.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **Nine Ways to Reduce Cognitive Load in Multimedia Learning** — Mayer & Moreno (Educational Psychologist 2003).
  - **Source:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232540768_Revising_the_Redundancy_Principle_in_Multimedia_Learning
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Practitioner-facing article distilling CTML research into a compact set of design moves for reducing cognitive load in multimedia instruction.
  - **Result:** Compresses the multimedia-learning research program into nine actionable design moves (off-loading, segmenting, pre-training, weeding, signaling, aligning, eliminating redundancy, synchronizing, individualizing).
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)
