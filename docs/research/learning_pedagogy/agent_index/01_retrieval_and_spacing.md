# Retrieval Practice and Spaced Practice

Synthesis of foundational papers on the two most-replicated findings in the cognitive science of learning: the testing effect (retrieval practice) and the spacing effect (distributed practice, interleaving, desirable difficulties).

## A1. Retrieval Practice and the Testing Effect

- **The Power of Testing Memory: Basic Research and Implications for Educational Practice** — Roediger & Karpicke (Perspectives on Psychological Science 2006).
  - **Source:** http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PPS.pdf
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Reviews experimental and educational evidence that retrieval attempts (tests) produce learning, contrasted with the conventional view that tests merely assess prior learning.
  - **Result:** Canonical synthesis establishing retrieval practice as a learning intervention; widely cited as the bridge from laboratory testing-effect studies to educational practice.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **The Critical Importance of Retrieval for Learning** — Karpicke & Roediger (Science 2008).
  - **Source:** https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1152408
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Experimentally separates the contributions of repeated study versus repeated retrieval on delayed recall of foreign-language vocabulary.
  - **Result:** Demonstrates that practicing retrieval outperforms repeated study on delayed retention even when learners predict the opposite; surfaces the metacognitive illusion that learners under-value retrieval practice.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

## A2. Spaced Practice, Interleaving, and Desirable Difficulties

- **Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis** — Cepeda et al. (Psychological Bulletin 2006).
  - **Source:** https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1771/
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Meta-analytic review of distributed-practice studies in verbal recall, integrating effects across inter-study intervals and retention intervals.
  - **Result:** Quantifies the spacing effect across a large body of studies; identifies the relationship between inter-study interval and retention as the basis for spacing-schedule design.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning** — Rohrer & Taylor (Instructional Science 2007).
  - **Source:** http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/~drohrer/pdfs/Rohrer&Taylor2007IS.pdf
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Compares blocked-practice schedules (all problems of a type together) against interleaved schedules (problem types mixed) for mathematics learning.
  - **Result:** Demonstrates that interleaving produces worse short-term performance but better long-term retention than blocking; canonical empirical demonstration of interleaving in mathematics.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **Memory and metamemory considerations in the training of human beings** — Bjork (in Metcalfe & Shimamura eds., MIT Press 1994).
  - **Source:** https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/1994-bjork.pdf
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Theoretical chapter analyzing how training conditions that slow acquisition can paradoxically improve long-term retention and transfer.
  - **Result:** Introduces the "desirable difficulties" framework, which subsumes spacing, interleaving, retrieval practice, and variability of practice; widely cited as the unifying conceptual umbrella.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)
