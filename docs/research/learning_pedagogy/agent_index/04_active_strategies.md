# Active Learning Strategies and Technique Efficacy

Synthesis of research on active-learning strategies (self-explanation), the meta-analytic literature comparing study techniques by efficacy, and the canonical refutation of the learning-styles hypothesis.

## D1. Active Strategies and Technique Efficacy

- **Self-Explanations: How Students Study and Use Examples in Learning to Solve Problems** — Chi et al. (Cognitive Science 1989).
  - **Source:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Empirical study of how students study worked examples in introductory physics, contrasting the self-explanation behavior of good and poor learners.
  - **Result:** Establishes the self-explanation effect: learners who generate explanations while studying worked examples acquire deeper understanding and transfer better than those who do not; foundational paper for the self-explanation research line.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology** — Dunlosky et al. (Psychological Science in the Public Interest 2013).
  - **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1529100612453266
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Meta-analytic review classifying ten commonly used study techniques by efficacy, evaluating each across criteria including generalizability across learners, materials, criterion tasks, and educational contexts.
  - **Result:** Provides an evidence-graded ranking: practice testing and distributed practice receive high-utility ratings; elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, and interleaved practice receive moderate-utility ratings; highlighting, rereading, summarization, keyword mnemonics, and imagery use for text receive low-utility ratings.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence** — Pashler et al. (Psychological Science in the Public Interest 2008).
  - **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Critical review of the empirical evidence supporting the learning-styles hypothesis — the claim that matching instruction to a learner's preferred modality improves learning.
  - **Result:** Identifies the specific experimental design required to support the learning-styles hypothesis (an aptitude-by-treatment interaction with measurable modality-matched outcomes) and concludes that the literature does not provide such evidence; canonical reference for the negative result.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)
