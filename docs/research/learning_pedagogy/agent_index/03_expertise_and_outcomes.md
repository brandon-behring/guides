# Expertise Development and Learning Outcomes

Synthesis of two adjacent theoretical lines: the cognitive psychology of expert performance (deliberate practice, chunking, pattern recognition) and the formal specification of learning outcomes (revised Bloom's taxonomy).

## C1. Expertise Development and Deliberate Practice

- **The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance** — Ericsson, Krampe, & Tesch-Römer (Psychological Review 1993).
  - **Source:** https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/0033-295X.100.3.363
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Theoretical paper arguing that expert performance arises from extended engagement in deliberate practice — specific, effortful activity with feedback — rather than from innate talent.
  - **Result:** Foundational paper for the deliberate-practice framework; defines deliberate practice as specific, effortful, feedback-driven activity aimed at improving identified weaknesses; the conceptual anchor for the popular "10,000-hour" heuristic (which the paper itself does not endorse as a universal rule).
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **Perception in Chess** — Chase & Simon (Cognitive Psychology 1973).
  - **Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0010028573900042
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Empirical study comparing chess masters' and novices' recall of board positions, contrasting structured positions (drawn from real games) against random arrangements.
  - **Result:** Establishes the chunking theory of expertise: experts recall meaningful patterns rather than individual pieces; pattern recognition rather than raw memory underlies skilled performance; foundational for later expertise research across domains.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

## C2. Learning Outcomes and Revised Bloom's Taxonomy

- **A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing: A Revision of Bloom's Taxonomy of Educational Objectives** — Anderson, Krathwohl, et al. (Longman / Pearson 2001).
  - **Source:** https://www.scribd.com/document/369062951/Lorin-W-Anderson-David-R-Krathwohl-A-taxonomy-for-learning-teaching-and-assessing-a-revision-of-Bloom-s-taxonomy-of-educational-objetives-Longman
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Book-length revision of Bloom's 1956 taxonomy, restructured as a two-dimensional matrix combining a cognitive-process dimension with a knowledge-type dimension.
  - **Result:** Canonical revised taxonomy: cognitive-process levels (Remember / Understand / Apply / Analyze / Evaluate / Create) crossed with knowledge types (factual, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive); widely adopted as the basis for learning-outcome statements in higher education.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)

- **A Revision of Bloom's Taxonomy: An Overview** — Krathwohl (Theory Into Practice 2002).
  - **Source:** https://cmapspublic2.ihmc.us/rid=1Q2PTM7HL-26LTFBX-9YN8/Krathwohl%202002.pdf
  - **Code:** —
  - **Mechanism:** Journal-length overview of the revised taxonomy presented for practitioner audiences.
  - **Result:** Compact accessible summary of the revised taxonomy's structure, intended use, and differences from the 1956 original; widely cited as the entry point for instructors learning the revised framework.
  - **Status:** Unverified. (no widely-known repo)
