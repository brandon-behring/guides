# Active Learning Strategies and Technique Efficacy

This file covers research on active-learning strategies (self-explanation, elaboration) and the meta-analytic literature comparing study techniques by efficacy. Includes the canonical refutation of the learning-styles hypothesis as a study-skills counterexample.

## D1. Active Strategies and Technique Efficacy

| Title | Authors (year) | Venue | arXiv/DOI | GitHub | One-line description | Key contribution |
|-------|----------------|-------|-----------|--------|----------------------|------------------|
| Self-Explanations: How Students Study and Use Examples in Learning to Solve Problems | Chi, Bassok, Lewis, Reimann, & Glaser (1989) | Cognitive Science 13(2) | https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1 | — | Empirical study of how students study worked examples in physics, contrasting good and poor learners' self-explanation behavior. | Establishes the self-explanation effect: learners who generate explanations while studying worked examples acquire deeper understanding than those who do not. |
| Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology | Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan, & Willingham (2013) | Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1) | https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266 | — | Meta-analytic review classifying ten common study techniques by efficacy across domains and learner populations. | Provides an evidence-graded ranking of study techniques: practice testing and distributed practice receive high-utility ratings; highlighting, rereading, and summarization receive low-utility ratings. |
| Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence | Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, & Bjork (2008) | Psychological Science in the Public Interest 9(3) | https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x | — | Critical review of the empirical evidence supporting the learning-styles hypothesis (matching instruction to a learner's preferred modality). | Concludes that the learning-styles hypothesis lacks supporting evidence; identifies the specific experimental design required to test it and finds it has not been met. |
