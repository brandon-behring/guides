# Retrieval Practice and Spaced Practice — Foundations

This file covers the two most-replicated findings in the cognitive science of learning: retrieval practice (the testing effect) and distributed practice (the spacing effect, including interleaving and desirable difficulties). Foundational papers from Roediger, Karpicke, Cepeda, Pashler, Rohrer, and Bjork.

| Title | Authors (year) | Venue | arXiv/DOI | GitHub | One-line description | Key contribution |
|-------|----------------|-------|-----------|--------|----------------------|------------------|
| Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis | Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted, & Rohrer (2006) | Psychological Bulletin 132(3) | (no arXiv) | — | Quantitative meta-analytic review of the spacing effect across verbal recall studies. | Establishes the spacing effect as one of the most robust findings in cognitive psychology; quantifies the relationship between inter-study interval and retention. |
| The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning | Rohrer & Taylor (2007) | Instructional Science 35 | (no arXiv) | — | Experimental study contrasting blocked vs interleaved practice schedules for mathematics problem-solving. | Demonstrates the interleaving effect for mathematics: shuffled (interleaved) problems produce worse short-term performance but better long-term retention than blocked practice. |
